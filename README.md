# The-Robot-Trick-Dog

An all mechanical, RX controlled mini robot trick dog whose main purpose is to u to controll it with a transmitter to see what cool tricks u can make it do.... (its purpose is not to walk in a straight line since then it would require code and wouldn't be fully mechanical anymore...)

## My build Process and how u can replicate it (replicate it just how i built it and u can always change some things.) --->

## 1. Make the body

I used some scrap foam board left over from my Flite Test P-51 Mustang build. Foam board works really well for this because it is pretty lightweight while still being strong enough for a tiny robot. I originally planned to make the robot with a completely flat body but then I decided to use some of the folding techniques I learned while building my RC plane. So instead of just making a flat piece I marked out the body on the foam board and cut it out.

<img src="https://cdn.hackclub.com/01a00b0a-aef0-7a0c-a10a-67c4e8709437/image.png" alt="Body template" width="25%">

Then cut along the lines you marked and fold the different sections upwards. This makes the body into a proper 3D shape instead of just being a flat sheet. Once everything was folded into place I used hot glue to hold the folds together.

<img src="https://cdn.hackclub.com/01a00b0b-64d7-71f7-a375-9158c1d73e61/image.png" alt="Folded body" width="25%">

## 2. Make the legs

Next I cut out four legs from some more scrap foam board. I wrapped the legs in electrical tape because it gives them a bit more grip while also reinforcing the foam.

<img src="https://cdn.hackclub.com/01a00b0c-db4e-72d7-b759-e4e29a855905/image.png" alt="Robot legs" width="25%">

<img src="https://cdn.hackclub.com/01a00b0c-f2c0-7942-9159-4c543aa1e151/image.png" alt="Legs" width="25%">

You can use different materials for the legs if you have something better lying around. Just keep them light because the servos have to move the whole thing.

## 3. Attach the legs to the servos

For the servos I reused the ones from my first RC plane. That plane had been through a LOT of crashes so I wasn't really going to use those servos for flying again anyway. I glued the legs directly onto the servo horns.

<img src="https://cdn.hackclub.com/01a00b0e-3a7d-7e3f-91bd-f16ba7349360/image.png" alt="Servo and leg" width="25%">

<img src="https://cdn.hackclub.com/01a00b45-a0f9-71a7-a695-699e6986d16b/image.png" alt="Attached legs" width="25%">

Make sure the legs are attached securely because the servo horns are going to take quite a bit of force when the robot moves.

## 4. Attach the servos to the body

Before attaching the servos I covered the body with duct tape. This was mainly so I could remove the servos later without ripping the paper layer off the foam board. I originally planned to use hot glue for attaching the servos but I ran out of hot glue sticks. So I used double-sided foam tape instead. And honestly it worked surprisingly well.

<img src="https://cdn.hackclub.com/01a00b48-3ba5-7e94-a4e7-57f414380b9b/image.png" alt="Servo mounting" width="25%">

<img src="https://cdn.hackclub.com/01a00b48-5243-7786-b8e4-9ec2752135bb/image.png" alt="Mounted servos" width="25%">

Try to position all four servos as symmetrically as possible. That makes the movement much easier to tune later.

## 5. Connect the diagonal servos

This is the part that makes the whole thing much simpler. Instead of controlling all four servos individually I connected the diagonal servos together using Y servo cables. The idea is basically:

- Servo 1 + Servo 4 → Y cable → CH1
- Servo 2 + Servo 3 → Y cable → CH2

The servos on each diagonal need to move in opposite directions. That way when one leg moves one way the diagonal leg moves the other way.

<img src="https://cdn.hackclub.com/01a00b4d-4788-70c8-97d0-586b3336caed/image.png" alt="Servo wiring diagram" width="25%">

I forgot to take a photo of the actual Y connectors so the diagram shows the basic idea. Then connect the first Y cable to CH1 on your receiver and the second Y cable to CH2.

<img src="https://cdn.hackclub.com/01a00b58-e4e6-7f0f-8a30-1c3f7306baaf/image.png" alt="Receiver wiring" width="25%">

You can then use the transmitter to control the two servo groups.

## 6. Clean up the servo wires

The servo wires are pretty long compared to the size of this robot. Mine were around 6 to 7 inches long. Instead of cutting them I just looped the excess wire and secured it to the body.

<img src="https://cdn.hackclub.com/01a00b4d-90ec-72be-bebe-d99e97c7b2fb/image.png" alt="Wire management" width="25%">

<img src="https://cdn.hackclub.com/01a00b4d-b05f-77c0-adba-6cc568e3b255/image.png" alt="More wire management" width="25%">

It isn't exactly the cleanest wiring ever but it works. If you're making your own version I'd definitely recommend keeping the wires out of the way of the servo horns and legs.

## 7. Power the robot

This part is actually pretty important. Four servos can draw a surprising amount of current. At first I tried to figure out how I could power everything from a small 3.7V battery but the battery I had wasn't able to provide what I needed. I also had a larger battery that could provide enough power but it had a 2-pin JST connector while my receiver needed a 3-pin servo-style connection. I didn't want to remove the JST connector because I needed it for another project. So I ended up using the setup I already had available. I used:

- 2200 mAh 3S 11.1V 30C LiPo
- ESC with an integrated BEC
- Servo extension cable
- RC receiver

The ESC's BEC provides the appropriate receiver and servo power from the LiPo. **Make sure the ESC's BEC output is suitable for your receiver and servos before connecting everything. Do not connect a battery directly to the receiver or servos unless the voltage is within their rated range.**

## 8. Keep the big battery off the robot

I didn't put the battery and ESC directly on the robot. There were three main reasons:

1. The battery is HEAVY compared to the robot.
2. The servos aren't powerful enough to move properly with that much weight on them.
3. The battery is actually longer than the robot's body.

Since this robot is mainly meant to do tricks in a small area rather than actually walk around a room this isn't really a huge problem. I just kept the battery off the robot and used the extension cable to give the robot some freedom to move. If you're making your own version I'd recommend using a much smaller power setup if you can. A suitable small battery combined with an appropriate voltage regulator and BEC could potentially let you put the entire power system directly on the robot.

## 9. Test the movement

Once everything is connected turn on the transmitter and receiver and test each movement slowly. Check that:

- All four servos move in the correct direction.
- The diagonal servos move opposite to each other.
- The legs don't hit the body.
- The servo horns don't collide with anything.
- The wires stay away from moving parts.
- The robot stays reasonably balanced.

If one servo is moving the wrong way you may need to reverse that channel on your transmitter or change how that servo is mounted.

## 10. Start experimenting with tricks

Once the basic movement works you can start messing around with the transmitter controls. Because the robot only has two controlled servo groups you can get some pretty interesting movements without needing a microcontroller.
